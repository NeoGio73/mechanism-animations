## The measured value: source and derivation

Experiment (water, 25 °C): cyanide addition to acetone, alkoxide formation. Equilibrium K = [R₂C(O⁻)CN]/([R₂C=O][CN⁻]), log K = −1.84, K = 0.0145 M⁻¹, ΔG° = +10.5 kJ/mol (+2.5 kcal/mol); rate = k[CN⁻][R₂C=O], log k = 0.60, k = 4.0 M⁻¹ s⁻¹, ΔG‡ = 69.6 kJ/mol (16.6 kcal/mol) (Eyring, 298.15 K, 1 M standard state, no transmission correction). All three measured values are in the tables above, next to the computed ones. Source: Schlesinger and Miller 1973 (G. Schlesinger and S. L. Miller, J. Am. Chem. Soc. 1973, 95, 3729–3735, doi:10.1021/ja00792a043), Table IV, acetone row: k₂ = 3.97 M⁻¹ s⁻¹ (CN⁻ + acetone → alkoxide), K₂ = 68.5 M (alkoxide → acetone + CN⁻; 1/K₂ = 0.0146 M⁻¹), k₋₂ = 272 s⁻¹ (alkoxide → acetone + CN⁻), giving ΔG‡ = 69.6 kJ/mol (16.6 kcal/mol), ΔG° = +10.5 kJ/mol (+2.5 kcal/mol) and ΔG‡(reverse) = 59.1 kJ/mol (14.1 kcal/mol). The same values, as log k = 0.60 and log K = −1.84, are compiled in Guthrie 1998 (J. P. Guthrie, J. Am. Chem. Soc. 1998, 120, 1688–1694, doi:10.1021/ja970506q, Table 1).

## Technical notes

- M06-2X underestimates this barrier: ΔG‡ = 72.1 kJ/mol (17.2 kcal/mol) at M06-2X-D3(0)/ma-def2-TZVP versus 79.3 kJ/mol (19.0 kcal/mol) at DLPNO-CCSD(T)/ma-def2-TZVP (7 kJ/mol lower). The measured value is in the table above, with the differences below it.
- Ion–dipole wells: the reactant complex lies 11.8 kJ/mol (2.8 kcal/mol) below separated reactants on the working-level curve but only 11.2 kJ/mol (2.7 kcal/mol) below at ma-def2-TZVP, and 8.1 kJ/mol (1.9 kcal/mol) below at DLPNO-CCSD(T)/ma-def2-TZVP. In water the deep double well of gas-phase ion–molecule reactions is largely washed out; what remains here is a shallow, partly artificial well (a teaching point, not a failure).
- Implicit solvent only: no explicit water molecules, so no specific hydrogen bonds to the nucleophile or the product anion.
- The product end of the path was truncated where the energy had flattened: the IRC was stopped by the IRC stopping rule (docs/protocol.md) at s = 4.41 bohr·amu½ (IRC step 94), after the bonds had finished changing; the product-end minimum was optimized from that last point.
- The reactant end of the path was truncated where the energy had flattened: the IRC was stopped by the IRC stopping rule (docs/protocol.md) at s = −7.89 bohr·amu½ (IRC step 158), after the bonds had finished changing; beyond it only the separating fragments drift apart. The reactant complex was optimized from that last point.
- One conformer only: the path starts from a single hand-built TS guess; other rotamers of the reactants were not searched.
- The canonical HOMO changes character abruptly along the path (near frame 148); canonical orbitals are delocalized and re-ordered by energy, which is why the localized IBOs are the default view.
- The path ends at the alkoxide, (CH₃)₂C(CN)O⁻, which lies above separated cyanide + acetone in free energy (ΔG = +26.3 kJ/mol (+6.3 kcal/mol) at the working level and +36.4 kJ/mol (+8.7 kcal/mol) at DLPNO-CCSD(T)/ma-def2-TZVP, 298 K, 1 M; electronically it is 22.8 kJ/mol (5.5 kcal/mol) and 12.6 kJ/mol (3.0 kcal/mol) below): the addition step on its own is uphill. The neutral cyanohydrin forms only after a later proton transfer (from HCN or water) that this path does not model.
- Implicit solvent under-stabilizes the alkoxide: SMD has no explicit water molecules, so the hydrogen bonds that water donates to the alkoxide oxygen are missing. Against experiment, DLPNO-CCSD(T)/ma-def2-TZVP puts the barrier 9.7 kJ/mol (2.3 kcal/mol) too high (79.3 against 69.6 kJ/mol) and the reaction free energy 25.9 kJ/mol (6.2 kcal/mol) too high (+36.4 against +10.5 kJ/mol). The barrier error is about 38 % of the product error, and the transition state is about 35 % pyramidalized at the carbonyl carbon (sum of its three bond angles 8.9° below 360°, against 25.2° below in the alkoxide; M06-2X/ma-def2-SVP geometries). This is consistent with hydrogen bonds to the developing alkoxide oxygen being missing in proportion to how far the alkoxide has formed. The test with added water molecules (section above) supports the missing hydrogen bonds for the reaction free energy, but the barrier does not follow this proportion.
- The working-level barrier, ΔG‡ = 67.4 kJ/mol (16.1 kcal/mol), is closer to experiment (69.6 kJ/mol) than coupled cluster only through cancellation of errors: with the same functional, the larger ma-def2-TZVP basis raises it to 72.1 kJ/mol (17.2 kcal/mol).
- The measured equilibrium constant rests on an estimated acidity of the cyanohydrin O–H (K₃ = 6.3 × 10⁻¹³, pKa 12.2, from a substituent-constant correlation, Ballinger and Long 1960, not a direct measurement), so ΔG° = +10.5 kJ/mol carries an uncertainty of a few kJ/mol. The forward rate constant does not depend on it. The reverse rate constant does: Schlesinger and Miller derive it rather than measure it, k₋₂ = k₂K₂ = k₂K′K_HCN/K₃ (their equation 24, p. 3734; 3.97 × 68.5 = 272 s⁻¹), so the reverse barrier from it (59.1 kJ/mol) carries the same uncertainty.
- Table IV of Schlesinger and Miller does not state where the acetone rate constant comes from. They cite Svirbely and Roth 1953 as the earlier kinetic study of acetone, so that is its probable origin; this is not confirmed.
- The measured rate constant refers to free cyanide (rate = k[CN⁻][R₂C=O]); observed rates depend on pH, because only CN⁻, not HCN, adds to the carbonyl.
- Reactant end of the path: the stopping rule ended the reactant side 0.94 kJ/mol (0.22 kcal/mol) above the optimized reactant complex, at an RMSD of 0.245 Å, while the energy was still falling slowly in this loosely bound complex. An optimization from that last frame reaches the same complex: −0.07 kJ/mol (−0.02 kcal/mol) from it, no imaginary frequency, all-atom RMSD 0.393 Å (heavy atoms 0.110 Å), the C(N)···C distance within 0.007 Å; it differs only in the rotation of the methyl groups.
